# TMDB as the film and series metadata source; we own only identifiers and user data

Film and series metadata (titles, posters, years, credits) comes from the TMDB API under its non-commercial licence. We persist only the External Reference of each catalog-backed Media Item plus data our Users create (Log Entries, Lists, Manual Media Items); TMDB remains the source of truth for everything else.

A catalog-backed series is a TMDB tv show, and its External Reference is (tmdb, series, the tv ID). It is one Media Item, the whole series, whatever its number of seasons: a season or an episode is never a Media Item. (Added 2026-11-15, when series were specified in #207.)

## Consequences

- TasteTrack must stay non-commercial: TMDB's licence forbids commercial use (for example charging users) without a separate agreement.
- The UI must show the TMDB logo and the notice "This product uses TMDB and the TMDB APIs but is not endorsed, certified, or otherwise approved by TMDB."
- TMDB metadata is cached, which the terms allow for up to 6 months; we use a hard expiry of 90 days, for films and series alike. The cache is disposable: the app must keep working if it is wiped, and if API access ends all cached TMDB content must be purged.
- Metadata comes in two languages, English and Ukrainian, and no others (revised 2026-10-19, when the interface switched from Ukrainian to English):
  - **Search** makes one TMDB call in the query's language: uk-UA when the query contains a Cyrillic letter, otherwise en-US. Results are shown in that language; a film TMDB has no translation for in that language comes back titled with its Original Title, not the English one. Series search follows the same rule with one call to `/search/tv`.
  - **Stored titles**: each cache entry holds the English Localized Title, the Ukrainian Localized Title when TMDB has one, and the Original Title, so the cache is keyed by External Reference alone, not by language. All three come from the single details call made on add: `/movie/{id}?language=en-US&append_to_response=translations`. The English title is the response's `title`, the Ukrainian one is the `data.title` of the translation whose `iso_639_1` is `uk`, and the Original Title is `original_title`. The `translations` list holds only real translations, with no fallback from another language, and an entry can exist with an empty title: an empty or missing Ukrainian title counts as none. (Checked against the real TMDB API on 2026-10-19: `append_to_response=translations` is supported, and both the search and the details `title` fall back to the Original Title, never to English, when the requested language has no translation.)
  - **Series titles** come the same way from one details call, `/tv/{id}?language=en-US&append_to_response=translations,credits`: the English title is the response's `name`, the Ukrainian one is the `data.name` of the `uk` translation, under the same rule for an empty or missing one, and the Original Title is `original_name`. A series' year is the year of its `first_air_date`.
  - **Display**: the English Localized Title first, falling back to the Original Title, with the Original Title and year alongside when it differs (e.g. "Amélie (Le Fabuleux Destin d'Amélie Poulain, 2001)"); the Ukrainian Localized Title, when present and different, as a secondary line ("Амелі").
  - Manual Media Items are User data with a title in any language and are shown unchanged.
- **A series' cache entry** holds, in the type-specific part ADR-0011 introduced, what that one details call returns: the backdrop; the first and last air years and the status; the seasons, each with its number and episode count, leaving out season 0 (TMDB's specials); the last episode that has aired, and the air date of the next one when TMDB gives it; and, for the About block, the overview and tagline in English and Ukrainian, the creators and the first 5 cast members. Whether a User is caught up is measured against the last aired episode, not against a season's episode count, because TMDB lists the episodes of a running season before they air. Covers, the 404 and 401/403 rules below and the TMDB credit are the films'.
- (Checked against the real TMDB API on 2026-11-15, for #209: `/tv/{id}` takes `append_to_response=translations,credits`, and its translations carry `data.name`, `data.overview` and `data.tagline`; `last_episode_to_air` and `next_episode_to_air` name a season and episode number, the next one null when TMDB knows of none; `seasons` lists season 0 when a series has specials, and a running season's `episode_count` includes episodes not yet aired, so MobLand's season 2 counted 10 episodes when the 9th had just aired; `/search/tv` and the details' `name` fall back to `original_name`, never to English, when the language has no translation. The last episode to air can be a special, of season 0: the last regular one is then the episode before the next regular one, or, once the series has ended, its final episode, and unknown otherwise.)
- The cache lives in D1, not KV: KV Free allows only 1,000 writes a day.
- Workers Free allows 50 external subrequests per invocation, cron runs included, so the cache is filled when an item is added (search result plus one details call) and there is **no refresh-on-read**. A Cron Trigger every 15 minutes refreshes a small batch of the oldest entries and detects Unavailable Media Items. A page fetches at most 10 cache misses and shows placeholders for the rest. Only items referenced by a Log Entry, List Item or In Progress (ADR-0011) are refreshed; unreferenced entries are deleted. Adding or refreshing a series costs one subrequest, as a film does.
- **Running series someone follows are refreshed daily.** The cron gains a third queue, between the entries filled from a search result alone and the week-old ones (MVP phasing below): series entries that some User has In Progress, whose status is neither ended nor cancelled (an unknown status counts as running), and that were last refreshed more than 24 hours ago. A caught-up User then sees a newly aired episode within a day. The run's budget stays 40 subrequests across all queues and sources (ADR-0011). The daily queue takes at most half of what the first queue leaves, so a deployment with many series In Progress still refreshes its week-old films and books, and it gets whatever the week-old queue leaves after that. An entry in two queues is refreshed once, and one TMDB doesn't answer for is marked as tried and goes to the back of its queue, as in the others. This queue refreshes sooner the entries the cron would refresh anyway, so it adds D1 writes only for those series, at most one refresh each a day instead of one a week (ADR-0006).
- Only a TMDB 404 counts toward unavailability, and only after 3 checks at least 24 hours apart with no success in between. 401/403 indicate an API access problem: they alert the Operator and never trigger conversion.
- Manual Media Items are User data, not TMDB content: they are never cached, never expire and are unaffected by a purge.

## MVP phasing

The MVP ships the cache filled on add (including the details call), the cron refresh and the D1 storage. The cron refreshes up to 40 entries a run: those filled from a search result alone first, at any age, then the oldest entries at least a week old, so a small deployment doesn't refetch the same films every 15 minutes. The daily queue for running series sits between the two (above). An entry TMDB doesn't answer for is marked as tried and goes behind the others, so entries TMDB never answers for can't take every batch. It deletes entries past the 90-day expiry that it couldn't refresh, and entries nothing has referenced for a day, which leaves time to log or plan a film just added from search. Reads enforce the expiry too, so it holds when the cron doesn't run: an entry past it reads as missing and is refetched like one. A referenced film with no entry is filled by the next page that shows it; the client loads the placeholders past a page's first 10 through `GET /api/catalog/items?ids=`, 10 at a time. Unavailable Media Item detection, auto-conversion and Operator alerts come later. Until then, an item TMDB deletes keeps its cached title only until the 90-day hard expiry.

## Judgement call: converting Unavailable Media Items

When TMDB stops returning an item and its title is still cached, every affected User gets their own Manual Media Item, and their Log Entries, List Items and In Progress move to it. Only bare facts (title, year) are copied into this user-owned data, never TMDB's descriptive content or images, for series as for films. A series' Progress stays with its In Progress, since it is User data, not TMDB Content. We treat a work's title and year as facts about the work rather than TMDB Content, so these converted items are permanent User data and survive a purge. Without this, a TMDB deletion would leave Users with ratings and Reviews attached to an unnamed ID.
