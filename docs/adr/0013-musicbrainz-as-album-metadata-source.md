# MusicBrainz as the album, song and artist metadata source; an album is the release group, a song the recording, an artist the MusicBrainz artist

Album metadata (titles, artists, years, types) comes from the MusicBrainz API, and covers come from the Cover Art Archive. A catalog-backed album is a MusicBrainz **release group**, and its External Reference is (musicbrainz, album, the release-group MBID). It is one Media Item, the work across all its editions (MusicBrainz's releases), so a remaster, a deluxe edition, a vinyl or another country's release is the same album, and a User's Log Entries for each are one Item History. MusicBrainz needs no key, and its core data is CC0, so it fits ADR-0001 and needs no licence of the kind ADR-0004 takes on for TMDB. Its release group matches ADR-0005's Media Item as an Open Library work does (ADR-0011). (Facts about MusicBrainz in this ADR come from its docs and live calls on 2026-11-16 and 2026-11-21, among them a check from a throwaway Worker; see `docs/research/music-metadata-sources.md`. Decided in #266. Revised 2026-11-22, when songs were specified in #279: they are in Songs, at the end, and the rest of this ADR is about albums unless it says otherwise. Revised 2026-11-23 in #304: song search runs in the browser, in Songs. Revised 2026-11-24, when artists were specified in #298: they are in Artists, at the end.)

## Considered Options

- **Deezer** was rejected. It is a store catalog: every remaster and edition is an album of its own, so one album would split into many Media Items. Its terms limit use to non-commercial purposes, and access can be revoked at any time.
- **The iTunes Search API** was rejected for the same split, and because its artwork may be used only to promote store content.
- **Discogs** was rejected because its search needs a personal token or OAuth. Its master is one album over its releases, so it could be a second source later.
- **Spotify** was rejected because its Web API needs a Premium account, and an app in development mode is limited to 5 users.
- **Last.fm** was rejected because images are excluded from its licence, so it has no covers.

## Consequences

- **Which release groups are Media Items.** A release group is a Media Item when its `primary-type` is Album or EP. Any `secondary-types` are allowed except Spokenword, Interview, Audiobook, Audio drama and Field recording, so a live album, a soundtrack, a compilation, a remix album and a mixtape are albums of their own, and a User rates the live album apart from the studio one. Search hides singles, Broadcasts and Other, as well as release groups with an excluded secondary type. A single is no Media Item: its songs are (Songs, below).
- **Requests.** Every request is a `GET` under `https://musicbrainz.org/ws/2/` with `fmt=json`. The base URL is Worker configuration, as Open Library's is: the real host everywhere except `local`, which calls the MusicBrainz fake.
- **Search** is one request: `/release-group/?query=(<q>) AND (primarytype:album OR primarytype:ep)`. The type filter sits inside the query, so the result limit counts only release groups of those types. Artist names in the query still match with the filter in place: "Abbey Road Beatles" and "1944 Jamala" find the right albums (checked 2026-11-21). The excluded secondary types are dropped by the Worker. A Cyrillic query is sent as typed and finds Ukrainian albums in their original script.
- **Ranking.** MusicBrainz ranks bare titles poorly: "Abbey Road" puts the Beatles third, behind an Italian jazz album and a compilation by Various Artists. Tribute and karaoke albums often outrank the original: "Pink Floyd Dark Side of the Moon" puts Pink Floyd third, and "Taylor Swift 1989" doesn't have Taylor Swift's album in its top 3 (checked 2026-11-21). The Worker reranks: first the release groups whose title and one of whose credited artists the query names in full (every word of each, articles aside), then those it names one of the two of, each word of the query counting for the title or an artist, not both; within each, Album before EP, release groups with no secondary type before the rest, then the ones with more releases (a search's `count`: an album reissued many times is the one people mean, and a duplicate entered by mistake has one), then MusicBrainz's own order. A tribute by another artist is often a plain studio album that names the original in its own title ("The String Quartet Tribute to Pink Floyd’s The Dark Side of the Moon"), so ranking by type alone put it above the original, and above a live original such as Nirvana's "MTV Unplugged in New York" (seen on staging 2026-11-21); naming the artist is what tells them apart. So the search field hints "Album and artist", and a search that finds nothing suggests adding the artist.
- **Titles.** A release group's `title` is the Original Title, and an album has no Localized Title: MusicBrainz holds few aliases for albums ("Земля" by Океан Ельзи has none), and album titles are rarely translated. Display follows ADR-0004's rules with one title. The artist credit is shown as MusicBrainz gives it, each credited name followed by its join phrase ("Bedouin, DakhaBrakha"), in its original script.
- **Facts.** The year is the year of the release group's `first-release-date`, the earliest release of any edition. The artist credit is the one above. The type is shown when the album isn't a plain studio album: an EP, or the secondary types ("2016 · Jamala · EP", "Live").
- **Details.** Adding an album is one request: `/release-group/<mbid>?inc=artist-credits+url-rels+aliases`. The album's part of the cache's type-specific `extra` (ADR-0011) holds the year, the artist credit as shown, the artists' MBIDs, the primary and secondary types and the release group's MusicBrainz URL. The artists' MBIDs are kept so that an album's page links to its artists without another call (Artists, below). An album has no About block: MusicBrainz has no description of its own, and Wikidata or Wikipedia as a second source is out of scope.
- **Covers** come from the Cover Art Archive by release-group MBID, `https://coverartarchive.org/release-group/<mbid>/front-<250|500|1200>`, which answers 307 to the image on archive.org, or 404 when the release group has no front cover. 2 of the 20 release groups checked on 2026-11-21 had none (Abbey Road Remix and The Bedouin Reworks Of Dakhabrakha). Covers are hotlinked from the browser, never stored, so they cost the Worker nothing. The Cover Art Archive grants no licence for the images, which are "copyrighted by their respective copyright owners", the same position as Open Library's covers. A cover that answers 404 or fails to load shows the album placeholder. Covers are square and are never cropped to 2:3. The Cover Art Archive base URL is Worker configuration too.
- **Identification.** Every request sends `User-Agent: TasteTrack/<version> (<Operator contact>)`, as MusicBrainz asks, with the version of the release and the Operator contact Open Library already uses (ADR-0011), read from Worker configuration, never from code. MusicBrainz needs no key, so albums add no new secret.
- **Pacing.** MusicBrainz allows each source IP about one request a second and answers 503 past it, or when its servers as a whole are busy. All MusicBrainz calls of one Worker invocation go through one queue: one at a time, at least 1.1 s apart. The interval is Worker configuration, so the local Worker uses a short one with a fake that enforces it. A 503 is retried once, through the same queue, and so is a merged release group's redirect (below). A page that fills several missing albums at once uses the same queue too.
- **Where search runs: in the Worker.** Workers share egress IPs, so other people's traffic spends the same limit. A throwaway Worker on `*.workers.dev` measured this on 2026-11-21, at one call every 1.1 s, with the User-Agent above and the repository URL as its contact. 11 of 20 searches got 503 on the first try, but only 1 of 20 (5%) still got 503 after one retry. 5 of 20 lookups got 503 on the first try, and none after the retry. #266 set the rule that search would move to the browser if more than 10% of searches still got 503 after one retry, so search stays in the Worker. A search that needs its retry waits at least 1.1 s longer, and one that gets 503 twice answers a "busy" error, and the UI says "MusicBrainz is busy, try again in a moment". If that becomes common, search can move to the browser without changing the data: MusicBrainz answers `access-control-allow-origin: *` and its CORS preflight allows a `User-Agent` request header (checked 2026-11-21). Chrome and Safari don't let a page set its own User-Agent, though, so browser calls would be known to MusicBrainz only by their Origin. Adding and refreshing an album stay in the Worker either way.
- **Cache.** Album metadata lives in the same D1 cache as the other Media Types and follows ADR-0004's rules: filled on add, no refresh-on-read, refreshed by the cron, and disposable. Its maximum entry age, the source property ADR-0011 introduced, is **none**: the data is CC0, and a cover's URL is keyed by the MBID, so it doesn't change when the cover does. An album entry the cron can't refresh keeps showing, so pages almost never fetch many albums from MusicBrainz at once.
- **Cron budget.** Adding or refreshing an album costs one subrequest, or two for a merged release group (below). Albums, songs and artists together take at most 10 of a run's 40 subrequests (ADR-0011, Songs, Artists), which is about 11 seconds of waiting in the queue. The week-old queue refreshes each referenced album about once a week.
- **Unavailable albums.** Only a release group MusicBrainz answers 404 for counts toward unavailability (it answers 404 for an MBID it doesn't know, checked 2026-11-21; how it answers for a deleted release group was not checked), under ADR-0004's rule of 3 checks at least 24 hours apart. A 503, a timeout or another 5xx is temporary and never counts, so throttling never converts anyone's albums. MusicBrainz needs no key, so a 401 or 403 means it is blocking us: it alerts the Operator and never triggers conversion. A converted album copies its title, year and artist credit.
- **Merges.** When MusicBrainz merges release groups, the old MBID answers `301` with a `Location` of the release group it became, and following it gives a `200` carrying the new MBID (checked from the Worker on 2026-11-21, for two merged release groups that Wikidata marks as redirects). The Cover Art Archive answers the old MBID with the new one's cover (checked from a home IP). A Media Item keeps the MBID it was added with and keeps refreshing through the redirect, and its stored External Reference is not rewritten. Two Media Items for one merged album are possible and accepted, since merges are rare. The source fetches with `redirect: "manual"` and follows a 301 itself, as a second call through the queue: `fetch` would otherwise make the second request at once, without the interval. A merged release group's refresh is therefore two subrequests.
- **Attribution.** CC0 doesn't require it, but the UI credits MusicBrainz, "Music data from MusicBrainz" ("Album data from MusicBrainz" until songs), with a link next to the TMDB notice and the Open Library and IGDB credits. Each album's page links to its release group on MusicBrainz, which is also the way to its track list and editions.
- **Manual albums.** An album MusicBrainz doesn't have is added as a Manual Media Item with a title, an optional Original Title, its artists and a year. Artists use the same field as a manual book's authors (ADR-0011).
- **No Progress.** An album is listened to in one sitting, as a film is watched, so it is never In Progress and has no Progress (ADR-0005).
- **Out of scope for now:** singles as Media Items, track lists and Progress by track, a description or About block, Localized Titles, streaming links, and re-pointing a merged album to its new MBID. Artists and their pages are in Artists, below.

## Songs

(Added 2026-11-22, when songs were specified in #279. Facts about MusicBrainz in this section come from its docs and from live calls that day, from a home IP; see the recording search check in `docs/research/music-metadata-sources.md`.)

A catalog-backed song is a MusicBrainz **recording**, and its External Reference is (musicbrainz, song, the recording MBID). It is one Media Item across every release the recording is on, so the album, the single and a compilation that carry it hold the same song, while a live version, a remix, a radio edit or another artist's cover is a recording, and so a song, of its own. A song is a light Media Type (ADR-0005): it is kept only in the Lists Users make.

- **Search** is one request, `/recording/?query=<query>&limit=50`, with this query, where `w1` to `wn` are the User's words:

  ```
  +((recording:w1 OR artist:w1) AND … AND (recording:wn OR artist:wn)
    AND NOT video:true AND NOT comment:live AND NOT comment:karaoke AND reid:*)
  secondarytype:compilation^5 secondarytype:soundtrack^5 primarytype:single^5 primarytype:album^5
  ```

  It is sent on one line. The words are the query split at whitespace, lowercased so that "AND", "OR" and "NOT" are never operators, less those with no letter or digit ("&"), each with Lucene's special characters escaped by a backslash, as in an album search. A query with no word left finds nothing and makes no request, so a name of symbols alone ("!!!") can't be searched by itself. A Cyrillic query is sent as typed.
- **Why that query.** Each of its three parts mends something the check saw fail:
  - **Every word is in the title or the artist**, so one request finds a song with or without its artist. Free text searches titles only, so the artist's words don't narrow it ("Шум Go_A" matched 3.4 million recordings) and a bare "Bohemian Rhapsody" didn't have Queen's in 100 rows, while `recording:(<words>) AND artist:(<words>)` found none of the 8 bare titles.
  - **The hiding is inside the query**, because MusicBrainz cuts to the limit before the browser sees a row. Without it, 87 of the first 100 rows for "Rock AND Roll Led Zeppelin" were live, and the studio recording wasn't among the 100.
  - **The boosts lift widely released recordings.** They change the order, never what matches. MusicBrainz's own order can put a much-released recording far down, and without them a bare "Bohemian Rhapsody" doesn't have Queen's in its first 100 rows.

  With all three, the song meant came first after TasteTrack's rules for 15 of 15 queries with an artist and 7 of 8 bare titles. The eighth, "Стефанія", finds nothing, as Kalush Orchestra's title is "Stefania". The limit is 50: 25 lost two of the songs, and 100 found no more while its largest answer was 1.5 MB against 0.9 MB, which matters on Workers Free's 10 ms of CPU.
- **Hiding** is three rules, the first two ignoring case.
  - The query leaves out video recordings, recordings on no release, and those whose disambiguation has the word "live" or the word "karaoke" anywhere. It can match only words, so "recorded live at …" is hidden, and a title with "live" in it ("Live Forever") is not.
  - The browser drops recordings marked as video, with no release, or with a disambiguation that starts with the letters "live" ("livestream", which the query lets through) or contains "karaoke".
  - After collapsing (below), the browser hides a one-release take: a row whose recording is on one release, counted by MBID, whose title is another row's plus a bracketed part at its start, its end or both, compared by words as ranking compares them for that optional part, when that other row has the same artist credit, by words as for collapsing, and is on more releases. "karma police radiohead" left 9 rows (checked 2026-11-23), 7 of them one-release 2019 takes from a bootleg of demos ("Karma Police {MD122}", "Karma Police (9.1)", "Karma Police (Mansfield)"), none with a disambiguation for the first two rules to see (#297). It now leaves the studio recording and "Karma Police (early)", which is on 3 releases. "Karma Police 9.1" goes too, as collapsing makes it one row with "Karma Police (9.1)". A take that is the only row for its title shows, and so does one on more than one release. The rule ignores the query, so a take stays hidden even when the query names it ("karma police mansfield radiohead"); it can be added as a manual song.
- **Collapsing.** Recordings whose title and artist credit have the same words are one row. Words are read as ranking reads them, lowercased, without accents, apostrophes or punctuation, but no article is left out; the credit's words are also read without "and", so "(I’ve Had) The Time of My Life" and "(I've Had) The Time of My Life" are one title, "Déjà Vu (Freemasons club mix) (no rap)" and "Deja Vu (Freemasons club mix, no rap)" another, and "Bill Medley & Jennifer Warnes" and "Bill Medley and Jennifer Warnes" one credit. A title or a credit with a word more or less stays a row of its own ("Time of My Life"). Comparing the title ignoring only case and the credit exactly left such look-alikes as two rows (#294). It is represented by the one on the most releases, then the one with the earliest first-release date (dated as for the cover, below), then the first in MusicBrainz's order. The row ranks by its representative's releases and by the place of the first of its recordings in MusicBrainz's order. A search result carries a recording's whole release list, so this needs no other request. The list repeats a release that has the recording twice, so releases are counted by their MBID. Under that earlier rule, collapsing took 343 of the check's 716 rows.
- **Ranking.** The browser reranks as for albums, without their types: first the songs whose title and one of whose credited artists the query names in full, then those it names one of the two of, each word of the query counting for one of them; within each, more releases first, then MusicBrainz's own order. A bracketed part at the start or the end of a title ("(I’ve Had) …", "… (Remastered)", "… {MD122}") is optional: the query names the title in full if it holds every word of it with that part or without it. "time of my life bill medley jennifer warnes" lacks "I’ve Had", so it ranked a 2008 "The Time of My Life" on 2 releases first and the 1987 "(I’ve Had) The Time of My Life" on 218 releases third (#295). A bracketed part in the middle stays: "Running up That Hill (A Deal With God) (2012 remix)" needs "A Deal With God". So the search field hints "Song and artist", and a search that finds nothing suggests adding the artist.
- **Titles.** A recording's `title` is the Original Title, and a song has no Localized Title, as an album has none.
- **Facts.** The year is the year of the recording's `first-release-date`. The artist credit is shown as for albums, in its original script, and the disambiguation as MusicBrainz gives it ("original version"). The album ("from Земля") and the cover follow the next rule. A song has no length.
- **The album and the cover** are chosen among the recording's Official releases, in this order:
  1. a studio release group before the rest: one with no secondary type, or only Soundtrack ("Help!");
  2. Album, then EP, then Single, then any other primary type, and of one type a plain release group, with no secondary type, before a soundtrack. "(I’ve Had) The Time of My Life" read "from Bevergem: Soundtrack" while it has a plain Album (#296). Putting plain first in rule 1 instead would put the plain Single "Help! / I’m Down" before the soundtrack Album Help!, and the song would show no album;
  3. the earliest release date, a missing month or day counting as the end of its year or month, and no date as last;
  4. the release's MBID, the lowest first, so that a tie is always broken alike.

  The cover is the Cover Art Archive's front cover of the first one's release group, by the albums' URLs. The album is that release group when it is a studio Album, and otherwise a song has none. With no Official release it has neither. A 404 or a failed load shows the song placeholder, and covers are square. Rules 1 and 3 both matter. Type and a plainly sorted date put a compilation on 5 of the check's 18 songs: "Обійми" came out "from Избранное", dated only "2013", instead of "from Земля". Rule 3 mends 2 of the 5, and rule 1 the other 3.
- **Details.** Adding or refreshing a song is one request, `/recording/<mbid>?inc=artist-credits+releases+release-groups`. The song's part of the cache's `extra` holds the year, the artist credit as shown, the artists' MBIDs (for the links to their pages, in Artists), the album's title and release-group MBID, the cover's release-group MBID, the disambiguation and the recording's MusicBrainz URL.
- **A lookup sees at most 25 releases.** MusicBrainz caps a lookup's linked entities, and filtering by status or type doesn't help. The album and cover are chosen among those 25, so they can differ from the search row's, which sees every release. They were the same for all 18 of the check's songs, but of 93 recordings with more than 25 releases the cover differed for 4 and the album for 3. This is accepted to keep one request. A search by `rid:` has the whole list, but it answers an unknown and a merged MBID alike, with no rows, where the rules for unavailability and merges need the lookup's 404 and 301. Browsing the releases would be a second request for each song.
- **Shared with albums.** A song's lookup uses the albums' queue at 1.1 s, User-Agent, base URLs, single retry on a 503 and "busy" error. Only a 404 counts toward unavailability, a 401 or 403 alerts the Operator, and a converted song copies its title, year and artist credit. A merged recording answers 301 (checked), which the source follows through the queue as a second call, and the Media Item keeps its MBID. The search rules, and the album and cover rule a lookup uses too, are pure functions in `src/shared/`, so the browser and the Worker apply the same ones.
- **Where search runs: in the browser.** The browser calls `https://musicbrainz.org/ws/2/recording/` itself, with the query above and `limit=50`, and applies the rules above to the answer. Covers link to `https://coverartarchive.org`, as for albums. The Worker makes no MusicBrainz call for a song search: `GET /api/catalog/search` answers 400 `search_in_browser` for songs, and the catalog source of songs has no search. Albums stay searched in the Worker; their CPU is measured in #306.
  - **Why.** #293 measured song search on staging on 2026-11-23, through the deployed Worker: 40 fixed queries, Ukrainian and English, with and without the artist, one every 1.5 s, as one User searching. 11 of 40 (27.5%) still answered "busy" after the one retry, over #266's 10% and the albums' 5%. `wrangler tail` gave a CPU time of median 11 ms, p90 20 ms and worst 74 ms, with 20 of 40 over Workers Free's 10 ms. Searches that found songs took median 13 ms, and those that answered "busy" median 6 ms, so parsing and ranking an answer of up to 0.9 MB is the cost. None was cut off, as all 40 ended `ok`. Workers share egress IPs, so the Worker spends a limit other people's traffic also spends. From the browser, each User spends their own IP's limit, and the Worker parses nothing. (The recording check had got 22% busy after one retry, but from a home IP and with no contact in the User-Agent.)
  - **CORS and identification.** MusicBrainz answers `access-control-allow-origin: *` (checked 2026-11-21). The browser sends no `User-Agent`, as Chrome and Safari don't let a page set one, so MusicBrainz knows these calls by their `Origin`. Whether MusicBrainz's policy accepts browser clients that send no `User-Agent` couldn't be checked: its wiki blocks automated reads.
  - **Pacing.** One queue per tab: calls at least 1.1 s apart, a 10 s limit for each. A 503 is asked again once, through the queue, and a second one shows "MusicBrainz is busy, try again in a moment". A search the User has already typed past is dropped from the queue, never sent. A timeout, a network failure or an answer of unknown shape shows the usual "unavailable". There is no fallback to the Worker.
  - **Marks.** Once MusicBrainz has answered, the browser makes one call to `GET /api/catalog/search/marks?mediaType=song&query=…&ids=<mbid>,…`, with at most 50 MBIDs, for the User's manual songs for the query and each row's Media Item and the User's marks, in the same number of D1 queries whatever the rows (ADR-0009). It merges them into the rows, so the rest of the UI sees the usual search results. A busy search shows no manual songs.
  - **Old tabs.** A tab still holding the bundle from before the move shows "unavailable" for a song search until it is reloaded, as there is no service worker to update it.
  - **Tests** send the browser's MusicBrainz and Cover Art Archive calls to the Worker's fakes through Playwright's `context.route`, the fake limiting each test browser on its own; on staging they reach the real MusicBrainz from the CI runner's browser.
- **Cache and cron.** Songs have no maximum entry age, for the albums' reasons. Adding or refreshing one costs one subrequest, two for a merged recording, and songs and albums share one allowance of at most 10 of a run's 40, filled in the order of the cron's queues whichever of the two an entry is, so a run waits no longer than albums alone could make it.
- **Attribution** becomes "Music data from MusicBrainz". Each song's page links to its recording on MusicBrainz.
- **Manual songs.** A song MusicBrainz doesn't have, or a live version that Search hides, is added as a Manual Media Item with a title, an optional Original Title, its artists, in the field a manual album's use, and an optional year.
- **Out of scope for now:** pasted streaming links, live versions in Search, Thoughts, Item History, Log Entries, Ratings, Planned and In Progress for songs, songs in the Library, a link from a song to its album's page in TasteTrack, a Like for songs, Cyrillic aliases and Localized Titles, length and track lists, and re-pointing a merged recording to its new MBID. Artists are in Artists, below.

## Artists

(Added 2026-11-24, when artists were specified in #298. Facts about MusicBrainz, Wikidata and Wikimedia Commons in this section come from their docs and live calls that day, from a home IP; see the artist search check in `docs/research/music-metadata-sources.md`.)

A catalog-backed artist is a MusicBrainz **artist** of any type, a person, a group, an orchestra, a choir, a character or another, and its External Reference is (musicbrainz, artist, the artist MBID). A solo project is an artist of its own, as MusicBrainz has it. Artists are a light Media Type (ADR-0005): it is kept in the Lists Users make and in their Favourites.

- **Search** is one request in the Worker, `/artist/?query=<query>&limit=25`, where the query is the User's words joined by `AND`: `w1 AND … AND wn`. The words are found as a song search finds them: the query split at whitespace, lowercased, less those with no letter or digit, each with Lucene's special characters escaped. A query with no word left finds nothing and makes no request. A Cyrillic query is sent as typed.
- **Why that query.** With no field, MusicBrainz matches each word against the name, the sort name, the aliases and the disambiguation, and it ignores accents ("Bjork" finds Björk). So one request finds an artist under either spelling, where MusicBrainz holds the other as an alias or sort name: "Okean Elzy" finds Океан Ельзи, and "Джамала" finds Jamala. "Казка" doesn't find KAZKA, which has no Cyrillic alias. Free text would join the words with OR: "Kalush Orchestra" matched 14,780 artists, every orchestra among them, against 1 with AND. In the 12 queries tried both ways, both put the same artist first, so AND only leaves out rows that share one word with the query. A word that is in neither the name nor an alias still hurts: "Pink Floyd band" puts tribute bands first. So the search field hints "Artist or band", and a search that finds nothing suggests "Try the name as it is spelled on the album".
- **Hiding.** The Worker drops MusicBrainz's special-purpose artists, by MBID:
  - Various Artists;
  - [unknown], [anonymous], [traditional], [no artist], [data], [dialogue], [language instruction], [theatre], [Disney], [church chimes] and [unknown animal];
  - MusicBrainz Test Artist.

  The research note lists their MBIDs, and the list lives in `src/shared/`. Nothing else tells them apart. Type Other is also some real artists' type ("The Beatles (Ukrainian)"). Five real artists are named "Various Artists". "No Artist" has the disambiguation "NOT a Special Purpose Artist". [unknown] came third for "Kalush Orchestra".
- **Ranking** is MusicBrainz's own order. Its score already ranks a name or alias the query names in full above names that share only a word with it. It put the artist meant first for every query whose spelling MusicBrainz knows. The exception was "Boombox", where the US duo of that name comes before Бумбокс. A solo artist and a group of one name came in a sensible order, the singer Alice Cooper before the band, told apart by their disambiguations ("the singer…", "the band…"). Unlike albums and songs, an artist has no title and credit to compare the query with, nor a release count to rank by, so the Worker doesn't rerank.
- **Search runs in the Worker,** as album search does. An answer is at most about 110 KB at `limit=25`, against up to 0.9 MB for a song search (#293), so parsing it costs little CPU. Busy answers follow the albums' rules: one retry through the queue, then "MusicBrainz is busy, try again in a moment". A search row shows the name, the type, the country, the years and the disambiguation, with no photo, so a search makes no Wikimedia call. How often an artist search is still busy after its retry from the Worker was not checked: from a home IP, 22% of the check's searches were, as for the recording check, while album searches from a Worker were 5% and song searches 27.5%. If it passes #266's 10%, artist search can move to the browser as song search did, since its rules are in `src/shared/` and MusicBrainz allows CORS.
- **Name.** An artist's `name`, in its original script, is the Original Title, and an artist has no Localized Title. Aliases and the sort name only help Search.
- **Facts:**
  - the type, as MusicBrainz gives it (Person, Group, Orchestra, Choir, Character, Other), or none;
  - the country, from MusicBrainz's `country`, an ISO 3166-1 code that is set even where the `area` is a city (DakhaBrakha's area is Kyïv, its country UA). #298 said "as an area", but an area can be a city or England. The client names the code in the UI's language with `Intl.DisplayNames` ("Ukraine", "United Kingdom"), and leaves out a code with no country name, such as XW (worldwide);
  - the years, from `life-span`: the begin year and a dash ("1994–"), with the end year after it when MusicBrainz has one ("1994–2015"). For a person they are the years of birth and death. With no begin year there are none. An artist MusicBrainz marks as ended with no end date (13 of the check's 725 rows, Queen among them) shows only the begin year and its dash, since the dash says no more than MusicBrainz knows;
  - the disambiguation, as MusicBrainz gives it.
- **Details.** Adding or refreshing an artist is two MusicBrainz requests, then the photo's Wikimedia calls:
  1. a lookup, `/artist/<mbid>?inc=url-rels`;
  2. a release-group search for the albums list (below), with the MBID the lookup answered with.

  The artist's part of the cache's `extra` holds:
  - the facts;
  - the artist's MusicBrainz URL;
  - the Wikidata ID;
  - the photo's Commons file name, author, licence and file page URL;
  - the albums list: each album's release-group MBID, title, year and primary type.
- **The albums list** is one release-group search, `/release-group/?query=<query>&limit=100`, with this query, where `<others>` is every secondary type but Soundtrack (Compilation, Live, Remix, DJ-mix, Mixtape/Street, Demo, Interview, Spokenword, Audiobook, Audio drama and Field recording), each a quoted `secondarytype:` term joined by `OR`:

  ```
  arid:<mbid> AND (primarytype:album OR primarytype:ep) AND NOT (<others>) AND status:official
  ```

  It keeps an artist's studio Albums and EPs: those with no secondary type, or only Soundtrack, as a song's album does (Songs, rule 1), with at least one Official release. The Worker applies the same type rule again to what comes back. The list is sorted oldest first by first-release date, a missing date last, then by title.
  - **Why not a browse.** #298 planned `/release-group?artist=<mbid>&type=album|ep&limit=100`, keeping those with no secondary type. A browse's order is neither by date nor by type, and the Beatles have 770 Album and EP release groups, so their first 100 left out A Hard Day's Night (104th) and Help! (106th). Keeping only release groups with no secondary type left out their four soundtrack albums and most of John Williams's film scores. The search filters before the limit, so the Beatles' answer is exactly their 22 official studio Albums, 1963–1970, and 16 EPs.
  - **Size and cost.** Only John Williams, of the 7 large artists checked, has more than 100 (236), and he gets the first 100 in MusicBrainz's search order, not the oldest 100, sorted oldest first. A search answer carries each release group's releases, so a large discography is a large answer: 524 KB for the Beatles, 300 KB for Prince, 32 KB for Океан Ельзи. JSON-parsing 524 KB took 0.7 ms on a developer machine. CPU time on Workers wasn't checked, and the cron's artist refreshes are where to look if a run is cut off.
  - **The index.** MusicBrainz's search index lags its database, so an album added on MusicBrainz reaches the list at a later refresh.
- **Album covers** in the list are the Cover Art Archive's front cover of each release group, by the albums' URLs, with no extra request. A 404 or a failed load shows the album placeholder.
- **The photo** is the Wikidata entity's P18 image. It takes two Wikimedia calls after the lookup:
  1. Wikidata's `https://www.wikidata.org/w/api.php?action=wbgetclaims&entity=<Q-id>&property=P18&format=json`, for the file name: the first P18 of preferred rank, otherwise the first of normal rank, never a deprecated one;
  2. Commons' `https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&prop=imageinfo&iiprop=url|extmetadata&titles=File:<name>&format=json`, for the credit.

  The Wikidata ID comes from the lookup's `wikidata` URL relation. 26 of the 26 artists the check's queries meant had a Wikidata link and 25 a P18 image. Only 1 of 13 lesser-known artists from the same searches had a photo, so the initials are a common case, not a rare one.
  - **Credit.** The author is the text content of `extmetadata.Artist`, an HTML fragment that can be a link to a Wikipedia article, a Commons user or another site, or plain text. Its markup is dropped and its whitespace collapsed ("Koh Hasebe; Distributed by Elektra Records"). The licence is `LicenseShortName` ("CC BY-SA 4.0", "Public domain"), and the link is the file's page, `descriptionurl`, which carries the licence in full. The credit line reads "Photo: <author> · <licence> · Wikimedia Commons" and links to that page. With no author it reads "Photo: <licence> · Wikimedia Commons". 20 of the check's 25 files required attribution, and none had an attribution text of its own.
  - **The image** is hotlinked from the browser, never stored, at `https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/<name>?width=500`. It redirects twice to the 500 px thumbnail on `thumb.wikimedia.org`, which answers with `access-control-allow-origin: *`. 500 px is one of Commons' standard thumbnail sizes: asked for 400, it served 500. A failed load shows the initials.
  - **Failures.** With no Wikidata link, no P18, or a Wikimedia call that fails, times out or answers in an unknown shape, an artist has the initials. That holds for a refresh too, with one exception: a refresh whose Wikimedia call fails keeps the photo and credit the entry already has, with the date they were read, so a passing outage doesn't blank a page for a week. A kept photo that was read more than 30 days ago goes back to the initials. A refresh that finds the Wikidata link or the P18 gone has the initials. The next refresh tries again either way. A Wikimedia failure never counts toward unavailability and never fails an add or a refresh.
  - **Identification and pacing.** Wikimedia calls send the TasteTrack User-Agent above, as Wikimedia's policy asks. They need no key. They don't go through the MusicBrainz queue, but one at a time.
- **Shared with albums:**
  - the queue at 1.1 s, the User-Agent, the base URLs, and a single retry on a 503;
  - only a lookup's 404 counts toward unavailability, under ADR-0004's rule;
  - a 401 or 403 alerts the Operator;
  - a converted artist copies their name.

  A busy album search after its retry fails the add or refresh like a busy lookup, as a temporary failure. A merged artist's lookup is taken to answer 301, as a merged release group's and recording's do. This was _not checked_ for artists. The source follows it through the queue as a second call, and the Media Item keeps its MBID. The search and hiding rules, the special-purpose MBIDs, the facts' formatting and the albums list's type rule are pure functions in `src/shared/`.
- **Cache and cron.** An artist entry's maximum entry age is **30 days**, unlike an album's or a song's, for two reasons. Commons files are deleted, renamed and relicensed, and a credit shouldn't be shown long after it was read. An active artist's albums list grows. The week-old queue refreshes each referenced artist about once a week, well inside the 30 days.
  - **Cost.** Refreshing an artist costs 2 MusicBrainz subrequests, 3 for a merged artist, and up to 2 Wikimedia ones.
  - **Budget.** Artists, albums and songs share one allowance of at most 10 MusicBrainz subrequests of a run's 40, filled in the order of the cron's queues. Wikimedia calls count toward the 40 but not the 10.
- **Manual artists.** An artist MusicBrainz doesn't have is added as a Manual Media Item with a name and an optional Original Title, with the same-title warning other Manual Media Items have, and no year.
- **Attribution** stays "Music data from MusicBrainz". Each artist's page links to the artist on MusicBrainz, and each photo carries its credit.
- **Links from albums and songs.** An album's or a song's page links each credited artist whose MBID it keeps to that artist's page. A manual album's or song's artists stay text.
- **Out of scope for now:**
  - Like and Favourites for other Media Types, numbered places in ordinary Collections, and a User-set size for Favourites;
  - Planned, a Backlog, Log Entries, Ratings, In Progress, Thoughts and Item History for artists, and artists in Saved and in Common Ground;
  - an artist's songs, and albums past the first 100;
  - live albums, compilations and other non-studio release groups in the albums list;
  - photos in Search rows;
  - Localized Titles, and an About block from Wikipedia;
  - telling a User about an artist's new releases, or subscribing to an artist;
  - re-pointing a merged artist to their new MBID;
  - pasted streaming links.
