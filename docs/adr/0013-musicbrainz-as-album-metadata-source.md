# MusicBrainz as the album metadata source; an album is the release group, not one release

Album metadata (titles, artists, years, types) comes from the MusicBrainz API, and covers come from the Cover Art Archive. A catalog-backed album is a MusicBrainz **release group**, and its External Reference is (musicbrainz, album, the release-group MBID). It is one Media Item, the work across all its releases, so a remaster, a deluxe edition, a vinyl or another country's release is the same album, and a User's Log Entries for each are one Item History. MusicBrainz needs no key, and its core data is CC0, so it fits ADR-0001 and needs no licence of the kind ADR-0004 takes on for TMDB. Its release group matches ADR-0005's Media Item as an Open Library work does (ADR-0011). (Facts about MusicBrainz in this ADR come from its docs and live calls on 2026-11-16 and 2026-11-21, among them a check from a throwaway Worker; see `docs/research/music-metadata-sources.md`. Decided in #266.)

## Considered Options

- **Deezer** was rejected. It is a store catalog: every remaster and edition is an album of its own, so one album would split into many Media Items. Its terms limit use to non-commercial purposes, and access can be revoked at any time.
- **The iTunes Search API** was rejected for the same split, and because its artwork may be used only to promote store content.
- **Discogs** was rejected because its search needs a personal token or OAuth. Its master is one album over its releases, so it could be a second source later.
- **Spotify** was rejected because its Web API needs a Premium account, and an app in development mode is limited to 5 users.
- **Last.fm** was rejected because images are excluded from its licence, so it has no covers.

## Consequences

- **Which release groups are Media Items.** A release group is a Media Item when its `primary-type` is Album or EP. Any `secondary-types` are allowed except Spokenword, Interview, Audiobook, Audio drama and Field recording, so a live album, a soundtrack, a compilation, a remix album and a mixtape are albums of their own, and a User rates the live album apart from the studio one. Search hides singles, Broadcasts and Other, as well as release groups with an excluded secondary type. Songs come in a later spec, and singles may come with them.
- **Requests.** Every request is a `GET` under `https://musicbrainz.org/ws/2/` with `fmt=json`. The base URL is Worker configuration, as Open Library's is: the real host everywhere except `local`, which calls the MusicBrainz fake.
- **Search** is one request: `/release-group/?query=(<q>) AND (primarytype:album OR primarytype:ep)`. The type filter sits inside the query, so the result limit counts only release groups of those types. Artist names in the query still match with the filter in place: "Abbey Road Beatles" and "1944 Jamala" find the right albums (checked 2026-11-21). The excluded secondary types are dropped by the Worker. A Cyrillic query is sent as typed and finds Ukrainian albums in their original script.
- **Ranking.** MusicBrainz ranks bare titles poorly: "Abbey Road" puts the Beatles third, behind an Italian jazz album and a compilation by Various Artists. Tribute and karaoke albums often outrank the original: "Pink Floyd Dark Side of the Moon" puts Pink Floyd third, and "Taylor Swift 1989" doesn't have Taylor Swift's album in its top 3 (checked 2026-11-21). The Worker reranks: Album before EP, and within each, release groups with no secondary type before the rest, then MusicBrainz's own order. A tribute by another artist is still a plain studio album, so the search field hints "Album and artist", and a search that finds nothing suggests adding the artist.
- **Titles.** A release group's `title` is the Original Title, and an album has no Localized Title: MusicBrainz holds few aliases for albums ("Земля" by Океан Ельзи has none), and album titles are rarely translated. Display follows ADR-0004's rules with one title. The artist credit is shown as MusicBrainz gives it, each credited name followed by its join phrase ("Bedouin, DakhaBrakha"), in its original script.
- **Facts.** The year is the year of the release group's `first-release-date`, the earliest release of any edition. The artist credit is the one above. The type is shown when the album isn't a plain studio album: an EP, or the secondary types ("2016 · Jamala · EP", "Live").
- **Details.** Adding an album is one request: `/release-group/<mbid>?inc=artist-credits+url-rels+aliases`. The album's part of the cache's type-specific `extra` (ADR-0011) holds the year, the artist credit as shown, the artists' MBIDs, the primary and secondary types and the release group's MusicBrainz URL. The artists' MBIDs are kept so that a later artists spec can link an album to its artists without another call. An album has no About block: MusicBrainz has no description of its own, and Wikidata or Wikipedia as a second source is out of scope.
- **Covers** come from the Cover Art Archive by release-group MBID, `https://coverartarchive.org/release-group/<mbid>/front-<250|500|1200>`, which answers 307 to the image on archive.org, or 404 when the release group has no front cover. 2 of the 20 release groups checked on 2026-11-21 had none (Abbey Road Remix and The Bedouin Reworks Of Dakhabrakha). Covers are hotlinked from the browser, never stored, so they cost the Worker nothing. The Cover Art Archive grants no licence for the images, which are "copyrighted by their respective copyright owners", the same position as Open Library's covers. A cover that answers 404 or fails to load shows the album placeholder. Covers are square and are never cropped to 2:3. The Cover Art Archive base URL is Worker configuration too.
- **Identification.** Every request sends `User-Agent: TasteTrack/<version> (<Operator contact>)`, as MusicBrainz asks, with the version of the release and the contact Open Library already uses (`OPEN_LIBRARY_CONTACT`, ADR-0011), read from Worker configuration, never from code. No key and no secret is needed.
- **Pacing.** MusicBrainz allows each source IP about one request a second and answers 503 past it, or when its servers as a whole are busy. All MusicBrainz calls of one Worker invocation go through one queue: one at a time, at least 1.1 s apart. The interval is Worker configuration, so the local Worker uses a short one with a fake that enforces it. A 503 is retried once, through the same queue. A page that fills several missing albums at once uses the same queue too.
- **Where search runs: in the Worker.** Workers share egress IPs, so other people's traffic spends the same limit. A throwaway Worker on `*.workers.dev` measured this on 2026-11-21, at one call every 1.1 s with the User-Agent above. 11 of 20 searches got 503 on the first try, but only 1 of 20 (5%) still got 503 after one retry. 5 of 20 lookups got 503 on the first try, and none after the retry. #266 set the rule that search would move to the browser if more than 10% of searches still got 503 after one retry, so search stays in the Worker. A search that needs its retry waits at least 1.1 s longer, and one that gets 503 twice answers a "busy" error, and the UI says "MusicBrainz is busy, try again in a moment". If that becomes common, search can move to the browser without changing the data: MusicBrainz answers `access-control-allow-origin: *` and its CORS preflight allows a `User-Agent` request header (checked 2026-11-21). Chrome and Safari don't let a page set its own User-Agent, though, so browser calls would be known to MusicBrainz only by their Origin. Adding and refreshing an album stay in the Worker either way.
- **Cache.** Album metadata lives in the same D1 cache as the other Media Types and follows ADR-0004's rules: filled on add, no refresh-on-read, refreshed by the cron, and disposable. Its maximum entry age, the source property ADR-0011 introduced, is **none**: the data is CC0, and a cover's URL is keyed by the MBID, so it doesn't change when the cover does. An album entry the cron can't refresh keeps showing, so pages almost never fetch many albums from MusicBrainz at once.
- **Cron budget.** Adding or refreshing an album costs one subrequest. Albums take at most 10 of a run's 40 subrequests (ADR-0011), which is about 11 seconds of waiting in the queue. The week-old queue refreshes each referenced album about once a week.
- **Unavailable albums.** Only a release group MusicBrainz answers 404 for counts toward unavailability, under ADR-0004's rule of 3 checks at least 24 hours apart. A 503, a timeout or another 5xx is temporary and never counts, so throttling never converts anyone's albums. MusicBrainz needs no key, so a 401 or 403 means it is blocking us: it alerts the Operator and never triggers conversion. A converted album copies its title, year and artist credit.
- **Merges.** When MusicBrainz merges release groups, the old MBID answers `301` with a `Location` of the release group it became, and `fetch` follows it to a `200` carrying the new MBID (checked from the Worker on 2026-11-21, for two merged release groups that Wikidata marks as redirects). The Cover Art Archive answers the old MBID with the new one's cover. A Media Item keeps the MBID it was added with and keeps refreshing through the redirect, and its stored External Reference is not rewritten. Two Media Items for one merged album are possible and accepted, since merges are rare. A merged release group's refresh is two requests to MusicBrainz, and `fetch` makes the second without the queue's interval: in the check both answered 200.
- **Attribution.** CC0 doesn't require it, but the UI credits MusicBrainz, "Album data from MusicBrainz", with a link next to the TMDB notice and the Open Library and IGDB credits. Each album's page links to its release group on MusicBrainz, which is also the way to its track list and editions.
- **Manual albums.** An album MusicBrainz doesn't have is added as a Manual Media Item with a title, an optional Original Title, its artists and a year. Artists use the same field as a manual book's authors (ADR-0011).
- **No Progress.** An album is listened to in one sitting, as a film is watched, so it is never In Progress and has no Progress (ADR-0005).
- **Out of scope for now:** singles, songs and artists as Media Types (later specs), track lists and Progress by track, a description or About block, Localized Titles, artist pages, streaming links, and re-pointing a merged album to its new MBID.
